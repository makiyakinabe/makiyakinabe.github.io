How it plays

  1. Step into their world

    Walk through a grandmother’s house, a hawker stall, a bedroom, a park, as them. Go wherever you like, at your own pace, and click whatever catches your eye.

  2. Read what they left

    Their phone opens up as you explore: messages, voice notes, photos, and an unsent message that slowly makes sense.

  3. Click to remember

    Click any object and it plays a short memory film, from its oldest moment to today, with the sounds of the life that happened around it.

  4. Choose how to remember

    Decide how to read each memory and what to do with their last words. There are no right answers, only your own.

    About the project

    The Last Chair is a set of ten short first-person stories about going back to the places people leave behind. Grief here isn’t a puzzle to solve. It’s personal, it doesn’t go in a straight line, and it keeps going. The idea at the centre: the people we lose stay with us through the spaces, objects, words and memories they leave behind.

    Ten kinds of grief

    • The Last Chair: an expected loss. A grandmother, and a house still full of her.
    • Left on Read: a sudden loss. A best friend, and an argument that never got resolved.
    • Closing Time: a parent who showed love through food and work, not words.
    • Still Here: ambiguous loss. A mother living with dementia, grieved while she’s still alive.
    • Long Distance: grieving from another country. A domestic worker in Singapore whose mother dies back home in the Philippines.
    • Four O’Clock: a first loss. A teenager and the family dog she grew up with.
    • Fifty-Two Years: late-life loss. A widower in his eighties learning to live without the wife who quietly ran everything.
    • Block 51: grief without a death. A family home and a neighbourhood lost to redevelopment.
    • The Top Bunk: losing a younger brother, and the guilt of being the one who grew up.
    • Visiting Hours: anticipatory grief. Saying goodbye to a grandfather in a hospice, while there is still time.

    Inspiration and research approach

    The project brings together two concepts: the domestic exploration of The Empty Chair and the phone-based memory fragments of The Last Message. The design draws on “continuing bonds” from grief research (people often stay connected to someone who has died through rituals, objects and stories) and on “ambiguous loss” (grief for someone who is physically present but psychologically changed). That’s why every ending is about what the player does with a message, not about getting closure.

    Ethical considerations

    • No graphic depictions of death, suicide or trauma. Each story states plainly how the person died or what is happening, so players aren’t left guessing about sensitive causes.
    • Each story has its own content note on the story screen. You can pause, skip memories or leave at any time. Nothing is timed and you can’t fail.
    • How you read each memory has no right answer, and no ending is presented as the best one.
    • You can finish a story without finding every piece of the message. Closure shouldn’t depend on having every answer.
    • The stories reflect Chinese, Malay, Indian, Eurasian and Filipino households in Singapore. The cultural details should be checked by people from those communities before a public release.
    • The fiction is kept clearly apart from the real support resources.

    Limitations of this prototype

    • The full game is planned as a 3D environment. This prototype uses illustrated first-person rooms with layered depth to test the stories, pacing and systems.
    • Voice notes are shown as transcripts. The humming and music are generated in the browser.
    • It hasn’t yet been playtested with bereaved people or dementia caregivers. That needs to happen before the full build.
    • The support resources are specific to Singapore.
